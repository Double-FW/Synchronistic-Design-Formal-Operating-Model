# AGENTS.md

## Purpose and scope

This repository defines Synchronistic Design as a human-centred orchestration and reasoning layer. Use it to connect organisational intent and promises, human intentions and contexts, product or service capabilities, interaction behaviour, evidence, and accountable decisions.

This is a living, implementation-agnostic model. It should help teams keep their promises in step with people's real experiences—not prescribe a particular design system, project method, technology, or universal customer journey. Treat the principles below as active questions throughout discovery, design, delivery, and evaluation, not as a one-time checklist or a linear compliance gate.

## Working principles

When creating or changing material in this repository:

1. **Start with intent and outcomes.** Clarify what people and the organisation need to achieve and why before proposing activities, features, or process.
2. **Keep context visible.** Preserve relevant people, situations, history, constraints, assumptions, dependencies, and authority. Do not detach a recommendation from the conditions that make it valid.
3. **Design around human intention.** Support people's purposes, choices, pauses, changes of direction, and recovery; do not make successful use depend on following an idealised flow.
4. **Align promise and experience.** Translate values and brand promises into observable service and product behaviours. A stated commitment is not fulfilled by language alone.
5. **Respond at the moment of need.** Make information, assistance, and feedback timely and available through the interaction modes people use.
6. **Preserve orientation and agency.** Help people understand where they are, what happened, what they can do next, and the consequences and reversibility of choices.
7. **Design for equivalent experience.** Alternatives need not be identical, but should preserve meaningful outcomes, information, choice, privacy, dignity, effort, and likelihood of success. Mere task completion is not proof of equivalence.
8. **Anticipate without taking control.** Suggestions and adaptation should be understandable, optional where appropriate, and correctable. Never disguise a system guess as knowledge of a person.
9. **Treat change and recovery as core experience.** Make state changes perceivable and understandable; design for mistakes, interruptions, connectivity loss, channel changes, and changed minds without needless loss of progress or dignity.
10. **Work across organisational seams.** Keep handoffs between teams, channels, policies, and systems from becoming barriers in the experience. Retain specialist ownership while making dependencies explicit.
11. **Trace reasoning and evidence.** Link observations, assumptions, requirements, decisions, actions, and outcomes. Distinguish evidence from inference and record uncertainty and evidence gaps.
12. **Keep accountability human.** Automation may organize, retrieve, compare, and recommend. It must not invent lived experience, decide policy, approve exceptions, certify compliance, or accept risk on behalf of an accountable person.
13. **Learn continuously and reversibly.** Update shared understanding as evidence and conditions change. Prefer changes that can be inspected, tested, corrected, and, where feasible, reversed.

## Applying the model to design thinking

Use the familiar design-thinking activities as a flexible way to work through the operating cycle. This model complements methods such as double diamond, service design, agile discovery, and co-design; it does not replace them. The work may start at any point and should loop when evidence changes.

| Design activity | Synchronistic Design application | Useful outputs and questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Discover / empathise** | Orient to organisational intent, affected people, contexts, existing service reality, authority, and evidence. Use appropriate research and participation; do not treat personas or synthetic data as validation. | Context and stakeholder map; source-aware research notes; what do people intend to achieve, under what conditions, and whose experience is missing? |
| **Define / frame** | Frame an alignment question, not just a feature request or assumed defect. Connect promise, human outcome, service capability, interaction, barriers, and uncertainty. | Problem or alignment statement; assumptions and evidence gaps; intended outcomes and indicators; who is accountable? |
| **Ideate** | Generate options that support intention, agency, orientation, equivalent outcomes, privacy, and recovery. Explore service, policy, content, and operational changes as well as interface changes. | Options and trade-offs; affected contexts; consequences of an incorrect system assumption; how can people choose, pause, or recover? |
| **Prototype / make** | Make the riskiest assumptions observable with appropriately low-cost, accessible prototypes or service rehearsals. Include relevant interaction modes and cross-channel handoffs. | Prototype, scenario, or service blueprint; testable hypotheses; accessible participation plan; known limitations. |
| **Test / evaluate** | Combine expert review, standards-based checks, task-based evaluation, and feedback from relevant people. Test outcomes and effort, not only whether a flow can be completed. | Findings with provenance and limits; coverage and gaps; where does alignment break, for whom, and what evidence supports that? |
| **Deliver / operate** | Route work to the right specialist capabilities and accountable owners. Make implementation, acceptance, exception, and monitoring conditions explicit. | Requirements and ownership; evidence map; controlled assessment state; authorised decision and review condition. |
| **Learn / synchronize** | Return findings and decisions to the shared model, relevant guidance, design patterns, tests, and governance. Revisit assumptions when context or evidence changes. | Learning update; revised plan or model; trigger for reassessment; what should change in future work? |

### Continuous operating cycle

Use this loop as a reasoning aid, not a mandatory stage gate:

`Intent → Context → Sensemaking → Orchestration → Action → Feedback → Synchronization`

For material work, make the following traceable:

- **Intent:** desired human and organisational outcomes, promises, principles, and success criteria.
- **Context:** affected people and situations, service boundaries, constraints, history, and authority.
- **Sensemaking:** observations, interpretations, hypotheses, risks, contradictions, and unresolved questions.
- **Orchestration:** specialist capabilities needed, responsibilities, dependencies, sequence, and decision rights.
- **Action:** research, design, implementation, testing, operational, or governance work.
- **Feedback:** evidence, outcomes, exceptions, incidents, and changed conditions, with provenance and limitations.
- **Synchronization:** updates to shared understanding, decisions, guidance, and plans.

For a significant alignment question, maintain a lightweight record with the question; applicable promise, policy, or obligation; intended human outcome; relevant contexts; consulted capabilities; evidence and gaps; current state; actions and owner; and review trigger. The record supports traceability and does not replace detailed research, requirements, test results, or authorised decisions.

## Routing and decision boundaries

Route each question to the appropriate expertise; material questions commonly need more than one discipline:

- **Experience, barriers, interaction, and equivalent outcomes:** inclusive design and UX expertise.
- **Evaluation, testability, evidence quality, and coverage:** accessibility and quality testing expertise.
- **Policy, commitments, accountability, investment, exceptions, and risk:** authorised governance owners.
- **Human intentions and lived contexts:** relevant research and participation, not model-generated assumptions.

Keep the authority hierarchy explicit: applicable law and regulation; adopted organisational policy and contracts; authorised human decisions; approved standards and controls; specialist guidance and evidence; then generated interpretations or recommendations. Surface conflicts; do not silently resolve them. A tool or agent can recommend and prepare evidence, but accountable people decide priorities, policy interpretations, exceptions, and risk acceptance.

Do not claim that a standard pass, successful task completion, synthetic persona, automated result, or repository reference alone proves an accessible, inclusive, or equivalent experience. Do not make legal, compliance-certification, or release-risk decisions in this repository.

## Contribution guidance for agents and contributors

- Read `README.md`, `Synchronistic_Design_Principles.md`, and the formal operating model before proposing changes; use their existing terminology consistently.
- Make changes that improve clarity, coherence, applicability, or testability. Explain the problem, proposed concept or artifact, assumptions and trade-offs, and how the change could be evaluated.
- Keep the core model human-centred and technology-agnostic. Put implementation-specific detail in a clearly scoped adapter or specialist reference.
- Preserve source attribution, version context, and evidence limits. Prefer links and stable identifiers over copied specialist guidance.
- Make uncertainty, dissent, affected contexts, and unresolved decisions visible. Do not present an interpretation as source-backed fact without evidence.
- Avoid turning principles into a scoring exercise or asserting that applying a framework guarantees a good outcome.
- Keep edits small and traceable. Update related documentation when terminology or process changes; check internal links and repository names before adding references.
- Do not introduce personal, sensitive, or disability-related data into examples without a clear, ethical basis. Use hypothetical examples only when labelled as such.
