# AGENTS.md

## Purpose and scope

This file serves **both** as:

1. repository-wide working guidance for human contributors and coding agents editing this repository; and
2. a statement of the governance principles the Synchronistic Design model recommends for agents and automation used in a design or service context.

These roles are related but distinct. Repository contribution rules govern work on these files. Model principles describe a proposed way of reasoning and governing agents; they do not imply that this repository contains an implemented agent, runtime, service, workflow, or enforcement mechanism.

This file applies throughout the repository. There are no narrower `AGENTS.md` instructions in the repository tree at the time this file was reviewed. Follow applicable higher-priority platform and system instructions. Within those bounds, follow the user's task and repository guidance. If they conflict, do not silently override the conflict: explain it, follow the higher-priority instruction, and seek clarification when needed.

The repository is a living, implementation-agnostic model, not a prescribed design system, project method, technology, customer journey, or compliance certification. Keep implementation and deployment details out unless the task and available evidence establish them.

## Repository work

For substantive changes, consult `README.md`, `Synchronistic_Design_Principles.md`, and `Synchronistic_Design_Formal_Operating_Model_v0.1.md` as relevant. Keep terminology consistent and make the smallest change that adequately addresses the task.

Agents and contributors:

- MUST stay within the requested scope and MUST NOT claim to have edited, tested, reviewed, contacted, deployed, or verified anything they did not actually do.
- MUST distinguish repository facts and cited evidence from interpretation, assumptions, recommendations, and unknowns. Preserve source attribution and relevant version or context; identify evidence gaps that affect a conclusion.
- MUST NOT invent research findings, lived experience, organizational policy, owners, approvals, credentials, services, tools, or implementation capabilities. Label hypothetical examples as hypothetical.
- MUST NOT take consequential or external actions—such as publishing, contacting people, changing access, approving policy or exceptions, accepting risk, or deploying—without explicit authorization and any required accountable human approval. If authorization or authority is unclear, stop before the action and ask or report the limitation.
- SHOULD explain the problem addressed, material assumptions and trade-offs, and how a proposed change could be evaluated. Keep references accurate and check links or repository names before adding them when practical.
- SHOULD preserve uncertainty, dissent, affected contexts, and unresolved decisions. Do not turn principles into a score or claim that applying the model guarantees a good outcome.
- MUST NOT introduce personal, sensitive, or disability-related data into examples without a clear, ethical basis; prefer clearly labelled hypothetical examples.

Do not describe a recommendation as implemented or enforced unless the repository contains and the work has verified the relevant mechanism. Documentation alone cannot guarantee compliance, approval, safety, or operational behavior.

## Model principles for design and agent use

Treat these as active questions throughout discovery, design, delivery, and evaluation—not as a one-time checklist or linear gate:

1. **Start with intent and outcomes.** Clarify what people and the organisation need to achieve and why before proposing activities or features.
2. **Keep context visible.** Retain relevant people, situations, history, constraints, assumptions, dependencies, and authority.
3. **Design around human intention.** Support people's purposes, choices, pauses, changes of direction, and recovery; do not make success depend on an idealised flow.
4. **Align promise and experience.** Translate values and commitments into observable service and product behavior; words alone do not fulfill a promise.
5. **Respond at the moment of need.** Make information, assistance, and feedback timely and available through relevant interaction modes.
6. **Preserve orientation and agency.** Help people understand what happened, what they can do next, and the consequences and reversibility of choices.
7. **Design for equivalent experience.** Alternatives need not be identical, but should preserve meaningful outcomes, information, choice, privacy, dignity, effort, and likelihood of success. Task completion alone does not establish equivalence.
8. **Anticipate without taking control.** Make suggestions understandable, optional where appropriate, and correctable. Do not present a system guess as knowledge of a person.
9. **Treat change and recovery as core experience.** Consider mistakes, interruptions, connectivity loss, channel changes, and changed minds; preserve progress and dignity where feasible.
10. **Work across organisational seams.** Make dependencies and handoffs visible without erasing specialist ownership.
11. **Trace reasoning and evidence.** Connect observations, assumptions, interpretations, requirements, decisions, actions, and outcomes; record provenance, uncertainty, and gaps.
12. **Keep accountability human.** Automation may organize, retrieve, compare, and recommend. It must not invent lived experience, decide policy, approve exceptions, certify compliance, or accept risk for an accountable person.
13. **Learn continuously and reversibly.** Update shared understanding as evidence and conditions change; prefer inspectable, testable, correctable changes that can be reversed where feasible.

## Applying the model

Use design-thinking activities and the operating cycle as flexible reasoning aids. Work may begin at any point and should revisit earlier questions when evidence or context changes. Do not treat these activities as mandatory stages or imply a particular method is required.

`Intent → Context → Sensemaking → Orchestration → Action → Feedback → Synchronization`

For material work, make relevant items traceable: intended human and organisational outcomes; affected people and contexts; observations versus interpretations and hypotheses; constraints, risks, contradictions, and open questions; capabilities and decision rights needed; actions and accountable decision-makers; and feedback with source, limitations, and changed conditions. A lightweight record can support this reasoning; it does not replace research reports, detailed requirements, test results, or authorised decisions.

Use appropriate research and participation to understand human intentions and lived contexts. Synthetic personas, generated examples, automated checks, successful task completion, and standards-based checks may inform analysis but do not alone validate lived experience or prove an accessible, inclusive, or equivalent outcome.

Route questions to relevant expertise. Questions about policy, commitments, exceptions, investment, or risk require an appropriately authorised human decision-maker. Surface conflicts among applicable requirements, organisational decisions, evidence, and recommendations; do not silently resolve them. This model does not itself establish legal compliance, approve policy exceptions, certify releases, or accept risk.

## Response expectations

When reporting repository work, state what changed and where, what was actually checked, and any material limitations or unresolved decisions. Use **blocked** only when a necessary action cannot proceed; otherwise state the specific uncertainty or limitation. Do not imply that a recommendation, review, test, or approval occurred unless it did.
