# Synchronistic Design Formal Operating Model

> Synchronistic Design as a human-centred orchestration and reasoning layer.

## Overview

The **Synchronistic Design Formal Operating Model** connects organisational intent and promises, human intentions and contexts, product and service capabilities, interaction behaviour, evidence, and accountable decisions. It helps teams coordinate research, design, delivery, accessibility, quality, governance, and learning without collapsing specialist responsibilities into one process.

The model is an adaptable reasoning layer between intent and implementation. It is not a prescribed project lifecycle, a particular design system, or a compliance certification method. It can complement design thinking, service design, agile delivery, and other established ways of working.

## Purpose

This repository provides a foundation for:

- translating purpose, values, and promises into observable experience outcomes;
- keeping human intention and context visible throughout design and delivery;
- coordinating people, specialist capabilities, processes, tools, and artifacts;
- making assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and decision rights explicit;
- connecting research, design decisions, implementation, accessibility evidence, and governance;
- maintaining alignment as evidence and conditions change; and
- turning distributed knowledge into coordinated action and learning.

## Core principles

1. **Intent before activity** — clarify the desired human and organisational outcomes before organizing work.
2. **Context is first-class** — preserve the relationships, circumstances, constraints, history, and authority that give information meaning.
3. **Design around human intention** — support people's goals, choices, pauses, and changes of direction rather than enforcing an idealized flow.
4. **Align promise and experience** — make organisational commitments observable in product and service behaviour.
5. **Explicit orchestration** — make roles, specialist contributions, dependencies, handoffs, and decision points visible.
6. **Equivalent experience** — preserve meaningful outcomes, information, agency, privacy, dignity, effort, and likelihood of success across different ways of interacting.
7. **Traceable reasoning** — connect evidence and assumptions to interpretations, decisions, actions, and outcomes; make uncertainty explicit.
8. **Human and machine collaboration** — use automation to extend judgment, not obscure responsibility or replace accountable decisions.
9. **Continuous synchronization** — update shared understanding and plans as new evidence and conditions emerge.
10. **Reversible learning** — prefer inspectable, testable, and recoverable changes where possible.

These are active questions throughout research, design, development, delivery, and evaluation—not a one-time checklist or a linear sequence.

## Applying design thinking

Use design-thinking activities as a flexible way to work through the operating cycle. Work may begin at any point and should loop as evidence changes.

| Activity | Synchronistic Design application | Typical outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Discover** | Orient to organisational intent, affected people, contexts, existing service reality, authority, and available evidence. Involve relevant people; do not treat synthetic personas as validation. | Context map, research evidence and limitations, affected groups and situations, unknowns. |
| **Define** | Frame an alignment question connecting human intention, organisational promise, service capability, expected experience, barriers, and evidence needs. | Problem framing, outcomes, assumptions, evidence gaps, accountable owner. |
| **Ideate** | Explore options across service, policy, content, operations, and interface—not only features. Preserve agency, equivalent outcomes, privacy, orientation, and recovery. | Options, trade-offs, hypotheses, impacted contexts, risks. |
| **Prototype** | Make important assumptions observable with appropriately accessible prototypes or service rehearsals. | Prototype or scenario, test plan, hypotheses, limitations. |
| **Test** | Combine standards-based checks, expert evaluation, task-based assessment, and feedback from relevant people. Assess effort, understanding, control, and outcomes—not only task completion. | Findings and provenance, coverage, confidence, gaps, questions for follow-up. |
| **Deliver and operate** | Route work to the relevant specialist capabilities and authorised owners. Define acceptance, monitoring, and review conditions. | Requirements, actions and owners, evidence map, authorised decisions. |
| **Learn** | Return evidence and decisions to shared understanding, guidance, patterns, tests, and governance. Revisit assumptions when context changes. | Learning update, revised plans or guidance, reassessment triggers. |

## Operating cycle

The model can be understood as a continuous loop:

```text
Intent → Context → Sensemaking → Orchestration → Action → Feedback → Synchronization
```

Each part should produce artifacts that make the next part more reliable:

- **Intent:** desired outcomes, promises, principles, and success criteria.
- **Context:** affected people and situations, actors, resources, constraints, history, and relevant signals.
- **Sensemaking:** observations, interpretations, hypotheses, risks, contradictions, and unresolved questions.
- **Orchestration:** priorities, responsibilities, specialist capabilities, dependencies, and sequencing.
- **Action:** research, experiments, design, implementation, decisions, and operational work.
- **Feedback:** findings, outcomes, exceptions, and changed conditions, with provenance and limitations.
- **Synchronization:** updates to shared understanding, decisions, guidance, and plans.

For material alignment questions, maintain a lightweight traceability record: the question; applicable promise, policy, or obligation; intended human outcome; relevant contexts; connected capabilities; evidence and gaps; current assessment; required action and owner; and review condition. This record supports—but does not replace—research reports, detailed requirements, test results, or authorised governance decisions.

## Routing and authority

Route questions to the relevant expertise; material questions often require multiple capabilities:

- **Experience, barriers, interactions, and equivalent outcomes:** inclusive design and UX expertise.
- **Testability, accessibility evaluation, evidence quality, and coverage:** accessibility and quality testing expertise.
- **Policy, accountability, investment, exceptions, and risk:** authorised governance owners.
- **Human intentions and lived contexts:** appropriate research and participation, not model-generated assumptions.

The operating model connects specialist knowledge; it does not override applicable law, adopted policy, authorised human decisions, or accountable risk owners. Surface conflicts and escalate them rather than silently resolving them. Distinguish evidence from inference, standards conformance from broader experience claims, and recommendations from decisions.

A successful task, automated check, standard pass, or synthetic persona alone does not demonstrate an accessible or equivalent experience. This repository does not certify legal compliance, approve policy exceptions, or accept release risk.

## Suggested repository structure

As the model develops, related material may be organized into areas such as:

```text
├── README.md                 # Orientation and operating principles
├── AGENTS.md                 # Contributor and agent guidance
├── principles/               # Foundational concepts and invariants
├── models/                   # Formal models, schemas, and representations
├── protocols/                # Coordination and decision protocols
├── workflows/                # Repeatable operating procedures
├── examples/                 # Worked examples and applications
└── glossary/                 # Shared vocabulary and definitions
```

The structure is intentionally adaptable. Additions should make the model easier to understand, apply, or evaluate.

## How to use this repository

1. Read the principles and formal operating model to establish shared language.
2. Describe the system being coordinated: its intent, affected people, boundaries, contexts, authority, and constraints.
3. Frame the alignment question and identify evidence, assumptions, and gaps.
4. Select the relevant specialist capabilities, model, or protocol; record why they are involved.
5. Make decisions, responsibilities, dependencies, and expected outcomes traceable.
6. Take action, observe the results, and update the evidence and operating approach.
7. Synchronize learning into the relevant guidance, tests, governance, and plans.

## Status

This repository is a living formalization of the Synchronistic Design operating model. Concepts, terminology, and supporting artifacts may evolve as the framework is tested in practice. The current formal model is a high-level draft; treat it as guidance for reasoning and coordination, not as a certification scheme.

## Contributing

Contributions should improve the clarity, coherence, applicability, or testability of the model. When proposing a change, explain:

- the problem or ambiguity being addressed;
- the concept, protocol, or artifact being changed;
- the assumptions and trade-offs involved; and
- how the change can be evaluated in practice.

Prefer small, explicit, and traceable changes over undocumented revisions. Preserve source attribution and evidence limitations, keep implementation-specific detail clearly scoped, and avoid presenting generated interpretations as evidence or accountable decisions.
